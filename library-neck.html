Neck

Neck pain & stiffness.

Cervical mechanics, posture, and the difference between muscular and joint-related pain — and how a chiropractor approaches it.

Why the cervical spine matters

The neck supports the head and protects the spinal cord. Most cervical pain is mechanical — posture, sleep position, screen time, or acute strain. The pain is real, but the cause is usually not dangerous.

Muscular vs. joint-related pain

Muscular pain feels like a constant ache or tightness, often worse with sustained posture. Joint-related pain tends to feel sharper and is associated with restricted motion — turning the head, looking up, or tilting becomes limited.

  • Where the pain is and what makes it worse
  • Range of motion and which movements are restricted
  • Whether symptoms radiate into the shoulders or arms
  • Whether imaging or a referral is needed

What conservative care looks like

Dr. Vecchioni is proficient in upper cervical techniques — Pierce Stillwagon, HIO — that address the upper cervical spine specifically. For most neck complaints, a combination of adjustment and rehabilitative exercise is appropriate.

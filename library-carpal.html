Upper extremity

Carpal tunnel.

Nerve compression at the wrist — what the doctor evaluates and how it's addressed.

What carpal tunnel syndrome is

The carpal tunnel is a narrow passage in the wrist through which the median nerve and several tendons pass. When the tissues in or around the tunnel become swollen or restricted, the median nerve can become compressed.

Common symptoms

Numbness, tingling, or burning in the thumb, index, middle, and half of the ring finger. Symptoms often appear during sleep or after sustained gripping.

  • Numbness or tingling in the fingers
  • Weak grip or tendency to drop things
  • Symptoms that wake you at night
  • Pain that radiates up the forearm

What the doctor evaluates

Dr. Vecchioni examines the wrist, forearm, and cervical spine to determine where the nerve irritation is coming from. Symptoms that look like carpal tunnel can sometimes come from the neck — a thorough exam is essential.

What conservative care looks like

If the issue is at the wrist, the doctor explains which technique fits your situation — typically a combination of wrist adjustment, soft-tissue work, and ergonomic recommendations. If the source is in the neck, treatment shifts accordingly.

When to call the office

Call (313) 277-1100 if your symptoms have lasted more than a few days, are getting worse, or are limiting your daily activities. The first call is for you to find out whether care makes sense for your situation.

What this article is not
